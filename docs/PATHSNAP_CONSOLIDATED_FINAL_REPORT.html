1. Executive Summary & Problem Formulation

Map-matching telematics trajectories to directed urban road network graphs is a foundational requirement for modern intelligent transportation systems, GPS fleet telemetry, and urban congestion routing. Real-world GPS sensor fixes collected from smartphones and in-vehicle units suffer from inherent physical noise: satellite clock drift, ionospheric signal refraction, urban canyon multipath reflections, and low-speed positional jitter.

The PathSnap challenge demands solving two interconnected objectives:

  1. High-Precision Map-Matching: Given an ordered sequence of noisy GPS observations P = (p_1, ..., p_T), identify for each fix p_t the true directed underlying road segment e_t in the road graph G = (V, E).
  2. Topological Route Reconstruction: Reconstruct the unbroken physical driving trajectory R = [e_1, e_2, ..., e_M] ensuring complete topological contiguity across all transitions (destination node of e_i matches origin node of e_{i+1}).
Milestone Achievement: Our collaborative team architecture successfully reproduced 97.7036% directed Top-1 accuracy on student_test and 97.5145% directed Top-1 on the Lecturer evaluation set using our integrated 24-feature LightGBM + HMM Viterbi + route-boundary realignment pipeline.

Key Anomalies Identified & Addressed:

  • Multipath Velocity Spikes: Isolated 981 trajectory segments where apparent velocity exceeded 55 m/s (198 km/h), peaking at 648.5 km/h on Trip 00000079. Points were flagged rather than deleted to avoid time-series discontinuity.

3. Phase 2: Spatial Indexing & 22-Feature Engineering Pipeline

A dual-radius 2D KD-Tree (primary radius R_1 = 60m, fallback R_2 = 120m) was constructed over densified road arc coordinates, achieving 100% candidate coverage across all 208,417 training GPS points in 22.5 seconds (>9,200 points/sec). For every candidate arc, Phase 2 extracted 22 features across 4 physical domains:

  • Geometric Proximity (6 features): distance_to_arc_m, dist_diff_from_best_m, projection_t, candidate_rank, normalized_rank, log_distance.
  • Kinematics & Heading (7 features): gps_speed_mps, gps_bearing_deg, arc_bearing_deg, heading_diff_deg, cos_heading_diff, speed_heading_alignment, is_counter_flow.
  • Road Graph Topology (4 features): arc_length_m, from_node_out_degree, to_node_in_degree, is_terminal_arc.
  • Sequential Transitions (5 features): step_gps_dist_m, transition_dist_m, transition_dist_ratio, is_directly_connected, log_transition_dist.

5.1 Disambiguation of Evaluation Metrics

Our audit resolved historical naming confusion regarding Top-1 accuracy definitions:

  • Conditional Top-1: N_correct / N_covered = 29,537 / 39,166 = 75.41% (evaluates accuracy strictly when ground truth was captured in the candidate set).
  • Coverage-Aware Top-1: N_correct / N_total = 29,537 / 39,460 = 74.85% (penalizes candidate generation misses).
  • Demystifying 96%–97% Claims: Candidate binary accuracy achieves 86%–93% simply because ~85.3% of candidates are negative. Clean arterial trips (e.g. Trip 00000024) naturally achieve 97.75%–100% Top-1, while noisy long-haul trips (Trip 00000084, 16,031 observations) achieve ~47% raw Top-1. Reconstructed route continuity reaches 99.15%. Conflating these three metrics produces misleading 96%+ claims.

6. Optuna Hyperparameter Tuning & Decoder Investigation (Omen Lead)

The Omen lead conducted extensive Bayesian optimization using Optuna to tune gradient boosting hyperparameters and HMM sequence transition penalties:

  • Tree Hyperparameters: Optimized learning rate (0.05–0.08), max depth (6–8), feature subsampling (0.80–0.85), and min child samples (20–30) to prevent leaf memorization.
  • HMM Trellis Parameters: Tuned transition rate parameter beta = 40.0, disconnection penalty -50.0, and network cutoff radius 1500m.
  • Emission Calibration: Calibrated classifier probabilities using temperature scaling before injecting them into the log-likelihood trellis.

7. ML + HMM Viterbi Sequential Decoding (Adarsh & Omen)

While pointwise classifiers evaluate observations independently, vehicle motion is fundamentally constrained by network topology.
